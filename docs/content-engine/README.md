# PoStory Content Engine

This folder carries forward the product discussion about turning an agency's client-provided media into grounded monthly content plans and editable short videos. It is intended as the starting context for implementation in another Codex conversation.

## Product direction

PoStory should help an agency do three connected jobs:

1. Understand the photos and videos an agency receives: what is shown, what is being said, which products and actions appear, and which moments can be reused.
2. Turn those verified materials and client facts into a complete monthly plan of 20 image-and-copy posts, with a calendar, varied creative hypotheses, platform fit, and review workflow.
3. Assemble selected video clips into short-form videos, with preview, timecoded revision notes, and a final export.

This is a content production and learning workflow, not a promise to make every post viral. The valuable outcome is more useful local content, tested against the client's actual goals such as inquiries, appointments, store visits, saves, and shares.

## Current business context from the conversation

- PoStory serves local agencies and small businesses, including windows and doors, discount appliances, and furniture.
- Local, qualified buyers matter more than large numbers of unrelated views.
- A C$599/month package was described as including 20 content pieces, a 30-day calendar, copy/design, review/publishing, and a monthly report, with two consolidated revision rounds. Treat these as the current discussion context and verify them against the live offer before changing product or contract logic.
- Do not promise virality, sales, or a fixed lead volume.
- Do not turn weekly performance optimization into unlimited unpaid rework.

## Important media handling

The sample videos were supplied privately through WeChat. No video files, thumbnails, absolute local paths, transcripts tied to a person, credentials, or platform account data are included in this public repository. The sample review contains only a short, generalized inventory and the editing lessons learned.

## Decisions already made

- Analyze the media before drafting posts or edits.
- Keep claims grounded in the supplied footage or client-approved facts. Mark missing information instead of inventing product specs.
- Generate meaningfully different creative angles; changing only the title does not count as a new creative hypothesis.
- Use a heuristic quality checklist internally, not a numerical “viral probability.”
- Separate organic content learning from paid-ad performance.
- Present a reviewable plan and preview before final render or publication.
- Preserve source assets and make edits reproducible from a timeline/edit decision file.
